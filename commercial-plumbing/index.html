If it is an emergency right now

Close the main water shutoff if water is flowing, and stop using fixtures if a drain is backing up. Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and from any area where sewage has surfaced.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility from outside. Do not turn lights on or off. Then call us once everyone is safe.

Restaurants, grease traps, and kitchen drains

Commercial kitchens send grease, oils, and food solids into the drain every hour they are open. A grease trap or interceptor catches part of this, but it has to be cleaned on a schedule or it overflows into the sewer line.

A kitchen drain that backs up during service is a common emergency call. We clear the line, find out why it filled, and help you set up a routine. For stubborn grease buildup in a main line, our hydro jetting service cleans the pipe wall instead of just boring a hole through the blockage.

Storm drains and monsoon season

Southern Arizona's summer monsoon brings short, heavy rain. Parking lots, loading docks, and roof drains carry a lot of water in a short time. Debris, sediment, and caliche-hard soil can block a storm drain or a roof drain and push water into the building.

Clearing debris before the season starts is cheaper than a flooded storeroom. We can inspect and flush roof and yard drains, and we will tell you if a line is collapsed or sunk, which a camera shows clearly.

Working with your schedule

We schedule non-emergency work for off-hours when we can, so customers are not disrupted. For property managers, we document what we find, with photos and a short written summary, so you have a record for owners and insurers.

A routine visit is cheaper than an emergency. A yearly check of water heaters, backflow assemblies, grease traps, and main lines catches problems on your schedule instead of the worst possible day.

Standards and rules for business plumbing

Grease interceptors are made to ASME A112.14.3, and the model to install is usually set by the plumbing code and any local rules for fats, oils, and grease. Those rules differ from town to town.
