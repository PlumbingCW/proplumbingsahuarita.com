Start with the meter: a ten-minute test

Shut off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker and any irrigation timer. Find your water meter, usually in a ground-level box near the street, and look at the low-flow indicator, often a small triangle or dial. If it moves with everything off, water is leaving the system somewhere.

Next, close the main shutoff at the house and look again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps running, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house. Tell us what you saw when you call and we bring the right equipment.

When a leak is an emergency, and when it can wait

Most leaks show up through a symptom, not a puddle.

  • ✓A water bill that jumped without a change in habits.
  • ✓A meter that moves with all fixtures off.
  • ✓A warm spot on a slab floor, or the sound of running water in a quiet house.
  • ✓A stain, bubble, or soft spot in drywall or ceiling.
  • ✓Low pressure that appeared suddenly, or a wet area in the yard between meter and house.

How we find the leak without opening everything

We use acoustic listening equipment, which picks up the sound of escaping water in pipes, and thermal imaging, which shows the temperature difference where hot water leaks into a wall or slab. For pressure-fed lines, we isolate sections and test, narrowing the leak to a short stretch.

The aim is to mark a spot on the surface and open only that spot. Where a leak is on the hot side under a slab, our slab leak repair page explains the repair paths.

The leak between the meter and the house

The water service line is the pipe that runs from the meter to the house. It is buried, you cannot see it, and when it leaks the first clue is often the bill, a soft patch in the yard, or low pressure. We trace the line, pressure test it, and listen along its length to find the break.

Repair options are a spot dig for a short failure or a new service line when the pipe is old or repeated breaks are likely. We call Arizona 811 before any dig so utilities can mark lines. Homeowners should do the same, since the service is free.

Irrigation, pools, and the desert evaporation problem

Drip lines, sprinkler valves, and backflow assemblies leak quietly, and a high summer bill is often blamed on watering when part of it is a broken line. We test irrigation zones to separate a leak from normal use.

Pools and fountains lose water to evaporation in desert heat even when nothing leaks. A bucket test, where you compare the pool's drop with a bucket of water set on a step, helps tell evaporation from a leak. If the pool loses more than the bucket, call us and we pressure test the lines.

Sahuarita homes and where leaks hide

Nearly all Sahuarita homes are built on a slab, so most hidden supply lines run in the attic, in walls, or under the concrete. That makes thermal and acoustic detection especially useful, since there is no basement or crawlspace to walk through. Pipe material depends on the builder and the year, so ask for your home records or have the lines checked. Owners in the Quail Creek community can read our Quail Creek page for tips on leaving a home empty for weeks.

Green Valley homes built from the 1980s onward may have older copper that can develop pinholes. If your home is older and the bill has crept up, ask us whether a whole-house pressure test is worth doing.

What the EPA says about household leaks

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, the average household's leaks can account for more than 9,300 gallons of water wasted every year. Common sources are worn toilet flappers, dripping faucets, and leaking valves.

Fixing them is among the least expensive ways to cut a water bill.

Safe steps before we arrive

These steps protect you and speed up the visit.

  • ✓Shut the main valve if water is flowing, and note the meter reading first.
  • ✓Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  • ✓Move rugs and valuables away from wet floors.
  • ✓Photograph stains, the meter, and any damage for your insurer.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
