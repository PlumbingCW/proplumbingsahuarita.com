What to do before the plumber arrives

A few steps in the first ten minutes limit damage and speed up the repair.

1

Stop the water

Close the main valve, usually at the meter box or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2

Make it safe

Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker if you can reach it dry. Never stand in water near outlets.

3

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, stop running water and flushing. More water only pushes the backup further.

4

Take photos

Photograph the damage, the meter, and any wet areas. Your insurer will ask for them.

5

Call us

Tell us what you see, where the main valve is, and how to reach you. We plan the visit from that.

Plumbing in a community that grew fast

Green Valley is an unincorporated community in Pima County known as a retirement community. Its population grew from about 5,700 in 1980 to more than 17,000 in 2000, so a large share of homes date from those decades. Pipes, water heaters, and drain lines from that era are now reaching the end of their service lives.

That age shows up in the calls we get: pinhole leaks in older copper, water heaters past their expected life, and tree roots in old sewer lines. If a home has had repeated leaks, ask about repiping. If drains slow down often, a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening in the line.

Winter freezes and the desert summer

Weather records for Green Valley show around 37 frost nights in an average winter. That is enough to crack exposed hose bibs, irrigation backflow assemblies, and pool equipment. Insulate or cover them before a freeze, and know where your main valve is.

Summer brings its own stress. Heat shortens the life of water heaters in garages and attics, and monsoon storms can send debris into yard drains. For permits on work in Green Valley, ask Pima County, since the community is not part of the Town of Sahuarita.
