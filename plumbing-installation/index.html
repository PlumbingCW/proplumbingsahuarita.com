What we install

Planned installation covers the work that goes in when you remodel a kitchen or bathroom, finish a space, or add a bath. We run supply lines and drains, set fixtures, and connect appliances like dishwashers, washers, and ice makers.

We also install new shutoff valves, pressure regulators, and hose bibs. These small parts protect a house, and a bad one can cause damage that costs far more than the part. If you are replacing a water heater, our water heater installation page covers it in detail.

Backflow prevention and your water supply

A backflow assembly keeps water from irrigation lines, pools, or other sources from flowing backward into the drinking supply. Some properties are required to have one, and some assemblies must be tested on a schedule.

Requirements depend on your water provider, so ask your utility what applies to your property. We install assemblies, repair them, and can arrange testing. Freeze damage is a common cause of failure, so an exposed assembly is worth covering before a cold night.

Gas line work: safety first

Gas appliance work has to be airtight. Every connection is leak tested before the appliance is used, and we confirm the shutoff valve is in the right place. Do not light a new appliance if you have any doubt about the connection.

If you ever smell gas, which is often described as rotten eggs, do not switch anything on or off. Leave, then call 911 and your gas utility from outside. For piped natural gas in the area, the utility is Southwest Gas. Propane customers should call their propane supplier.

Permits, inspections, and Arizona 811

Some plumbing work needs a permit and an inspection. Ask the Town of Sahuarita Building Safety department whether your project does, since requirements depend on the scope of the work. We tell you what we know about your project, and you confirm the rules with the Town.

Before any digging, such as a new outdoor line, call Arizona 811. The service is free and utilities mark buried lines so a shovel does not hit a gas or water line. It is the law in Arizona and it protects you.

Standards that guide new plumbing work

Gas piping follows NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and reduced pressure backflow assemblies are made to ASSE 1013. Fixtures and pipe carry markings for the standards they meet.

The plumbing code edition and local amendments for your project come from your building department, and we confirm them with the building safety department before work begins.
