Repiping in Sahuarita, AZ

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a house when repairs have stopped making sense. In Sahuarita and Green Valley that usually means pinhole leaks in aging copper, brittle plastic, or old galvanized lines that have narrowed.

If you have an active leak, start with emergency burst pipe repair. If the same lines keep failing, this page is the planned route.

When repiping beats repeated repairs

One leak in a healthy system is a repair. Two or three leaks in a year, or a leak that returns elsewhere after each fix, signals that the pipe has reached the end of its life. At that point, each repair costs money and risks damage, and a repipe is cheaper over time.

Low pressure and discolored water can also point to narrowing from scale or corrosion. We start with a pressure test and an inspection of the accessible lines before recommending anything.

Signs your home may need a repipe

Look for patterns, not single events.

  • ✓Repeated pinhole leaks in copper, often green stains on pipe or wet spots in walls.
  • ✓Rust-brown or flaking water from galvanized steel supply lines.
  • ✓Low pressure that persists after fixtures and regulator are checked.
  • ✓Brittle plastic supply lines that crack when touched or repaired.
  • ✓A home from the era of polybutylene, gray plastic supply pipe used in some homes built from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s.

What materials homes in the area have

Green Valley grew fast between 1980 and 2000, when its population grew from about 5,700 to more than 17,000. Homes from that period may carry original copper, and some builders used polybutylene during the same era. For newer homes, the year built and the builder are the best clues to what is in the walls.

What a whole-house repipe involves

A repipe is a multi-day project. We shut off the water, run new lines through the attic, walls, or crawl areas, connect each fixture, and restore service. Drywall access holes are patched, though painting is separate.

Because most Sahuarita homes are on a slab, we prefer to reroute through the attic where the layout allows. That avoids breaking concrete. See slab leak repair for how this works when the leak is below the floor.

Planning around a multi-day project

Before work starts, we walk the house and agree on the plan. Cover or move furniture near walls where access holes will go, clear the area around the water heater, and tell us about pets and anyone who works from home. Small steps like these keep the project moving.

We also ask where the water shutoffs are and test them. A main valve that does not close fully is replaced first, because it protects the house during every later repair.

Pipe standards that matter

Replacement pipe should meet a published standard, and the marking printed on the pipe tells you which one. PEX tubing, for example, is made to ASTM F876, and copper and CPVC have standards of their own.

A repipe is covered by the plumbing code your jurisdiction has adopted and often needs a permit. Ask the building safety department what applies.

Safety checklist for repipe week

A little preparation makes the work safer for everyone.

  • ✓Clear access to the water heater, the main valve, and any walls where we will open drywall.
  • ✓Move furniture, art, and electronics away from work areas.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of the work zone.
  • ✓Tell us about anyone with health needs who relies on running water.
  • ✓If you ever smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility from outside.

Repiping questions from Sahuarita homeowners

How long does a repipe take?

Most whole-house repipes take a few days, and we plan so water is restored each evening where possible.

Do I need to move out during a repipe?

Usually not. Water is off for part of each day, and we tell you the schedule ahead of time.

Is PEX safe and durable?

PEX is widely used in residential plumbing and has a long track record when installed correctly and protected from sunlight.

Can I repipe only part of the house?

Yes. If one section is failing, we may recommend replacing only that run. We explain the trade-off.
