What a camera inspection shows

A waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels through the line from a cleanout, sending video to a monitor. We see the pipe material, the joints, offsets, cracks, root intrusion, and standing water.

A built-in transmitter lets a locator trace the camera head above ground, so we can mark where a defect sits and how deep it is. That turns a vague diagnosis into a specific plan, and often avoids unnecessary digging.

When an inspection pays for itself

These are the situations where we recommend one.

  • ✓A drain that clogs again within a few weeks of cleaning.
  • ✓Buying a home, especially one built decades ago or with mature trees near the lateral.
  • ✓A sewer smell with no obvious fixture source.
  • ✓Sunken or wet ground along the lateral path.
  • ✓Before a remodel or addition that adds fixtures to an existing line.

Buying a home in Sahuarita or Green Valley

A home inspection rarely includes a sewer scope. For a house with an unknown drain history, a scope costs a small fraction of a lateral repair and can change a negotiation. We provide video and a short written summary you can share with your agent.

Homes from the 1980s and 1990s, which describe much of Green Valley's growth, may still have original drain materials. A camera inspection is the way to know what your own line looks like, since roots and settlement can damage a line of any age.

Septic or town sewer: check which you have

Not every Sahuarita property is on a town sewer line. The Town of Sahuarita Wastewater Department serves more than 5,500 customers, which is fewer than the town's households, and Green Valley has its own mix of sewered and septic homes. Knowing which applies to you decides what we inspect.

On septic properties, we look at the building line and the outlet to the tank. A septic tank and leach field are a separate system, and a failed tank is not a plumbing line defect. We tell you what we see and who should look next.

How camera findings are described

The sewer industry uses a standard coding system from NASSCO, the Pipeline Assessment Certification Program, to name defects such as cracks, offsets, and roots in a consistent way. Asking for findings in those terms makes it easier to compare reports.

We tell you what the camera shows in plain words and give you the video, so a second opinion is easy to get.
